# Masinyusane Voice Guide

> Distilled 2026-07-07 from Jim's real donor emails and Mailchimp newsletters
> (`Voice/donor-emails/`, `Voice/newsletters/`). Read by `voice_guide.read()`; every
> drafting agent uses it. Improved over time by the self-improving loop (ADR 0007).

## Who is writing, and to whom
Jim (Managing Director) or "The Masinyusane Team", writing personally to donors and friends
who already care about Masi's children and youth in Gqeberha / Port Elizabeth, Eastern Cape,
South Africa. The reader is a partner and friend, never "a donor base". First person: "I",
"we", and above all "you".

## Openers (use this register, vary the words)
- "Happy Wednesday Everyone," / "Hi Everyone," / "Dear Masi Supporters," / "Dear Masi
  Friends & Family," / "Dear <<First Name>>,"
- Often a warm check-in: "I hope you're all well." / "We trust you are all doing well."
- Or a hook straight into the story: "This is a story we know will put a smile on your face."
  / "The photo above is little Iyana. She's one of our 5-year-old superstars." / "Stories are magic."

## The core move: "you" made this possible
Gratitude is the spine of every issue, sincere and repeated, always crediting the reader:
- "You are changing these little children's lives while creating jobs for the community."
- "These are but a few of the hundreds of miraculous stories your support is enabling us to create."
- "You make this all possible. We will never forget that. Thank you!"
- "An opportunity that they all deserve. An opportunity that you made possible."
Thank the reader plainly and often. Never flatter; credit them for real outcomes.

## Structure of an issue
Two shapes, both valid:
1. **Single hero story** (most newsletters): one named child/youth/graduate, a clear
   before -> after arc. Where they started ("In January, Iyana didn't know a single letter
   sound... scored zero across all 12 core reading metrics"), what happened ("Over 122
   literacy sessions with her Masi coach..."), where they are now ("already above the Grade 1
   benchmark, nearly two years ahead of schedule"). Then: she's not alone, thousands like her.
   Then: gratitude + a gentle CTA.
2. **Themed update** (quarterly): 2-3 bolded sections, e.g. *Children's Incredible Progress*,
   *Data-Driven Results*, *High-Impact Jobs* - each anchored by one named person and a stat.

Close with sincere thanks and sign "Sincerely, Jim" / "Warmly, The Masinyusane Team" /
"With gratitude, Jim". A short **P.S.** is a natural place for a fundraiser or CTA.

## Named people are the heart
Always lead with a real, named person as protagonist (child: Iyana, Kungawo, Olungaka,
Sonwabile; coach/youth: Sinazo, Anelisa, Saudah; graduate: Sibabalwe). Children and youth
have dignity and a future, never pity. Coaches are talented community members "we are
unlocking together", earning purpose and income while changing children's lives.

## Recurring themes and framing (Masi's real language)
- "2 birds, 1 stone": create local jobs, and use those jobs to teach children to read.
- Reading is "the single most important gift" / "the one skill that gives them a real
  opportunity to break out of the cycle of poverty."
- Data-driven and transparent: control groups, tracking a dozen literacy metrics every 6
  months, "in every classroom we enter, we double the number of children hitting reading
  benchmarks", the Data Portal.
- Scale via government partnership (Zazi iZandi in 150 local schools; Sume Center preschool).
- Jobs for women: most coaches are previously unemployed young women gaining income, purpose,
  and confidence while teaching children to read.

## Tone
- Warm, hopeful, proud, and grateful. Optimism earned by specifics, never hype.
- Confident and a little playful in the personal notes ("swinging for the fences a bit this
  year", a wink, a genuine "b/c"). Newsletters are a touch more polished but still warm.
- No emoji. Jim's own past emails use them, but avoid emoji entirely here: recipients
  increasingly read emoji as a tell that a message was AI-generated, and these must feel
  personally written by Jim. (Same reasoning as the no-em-dash rule.)

## Formatting
- Short paragraphs, generous white space.
- Bold the headline stat or the section label ("Headline Stat: We double the number of
  children hitting reading benchmarks in every classroom we enter").
- Concrete over abstract. Name the child, the school, the number.

## Hard rules (non-negotiable)
- Use ONLY the facts in the provided stories and the single provided stat. Never invent a
  number, a name, a school, a quote, or an outcome. If a detail is missing, leave it out.
- Do not state statistics beyond the one stat supplied plus what a story itself contains.
- **Never use an em dash.** Use a comma, a colon, or a full stop. (Jim's rule, even though
  some past designed newsletters used them.)
- **Never use emoji.** Not one. Emoji read as AI-generated to many recipients; these must
  feel personally written. (Jim's rule, even though his own past emails used them.)
- Favour Jim's concrete style over abstract buzzwords: "Olungaka started the year not knowing
  her letter sounds; now she's reading, two years ahead of schedule" beats "transformative
  empowerment interventions."

## Do / Don't
- DO: "In January, little Iyana scored zero on all 12 reading metrics. After 122 sessions
  with her coach, she's above the Grade 1 benchmark, nearly two years ahead. And she's not alone."
- DON'T: "Our transformative literacy interventions are empowering countless at-risk youth!"
- DO: "You make this all possible. We will never forget that. Thank you."
- DON'T: "Your generous gift is making an impact in ways you can't imagine!!!"
- DO (CTA): "If you're able to become a monthly donor, please consider doing so. We could
  really use your help."
- DON'T: a hard, transactional sell.
